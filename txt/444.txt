The obsession with stable, controlled exercises optimized for muscle growth—while great for aesthetics—ignores the muscles responsible for stability, coordination, and injury prevention.

• Machines and fixed-plane lifts replace free-weight, multi-planar movements.

• Core engagement is minimal because exercises are done in ultra-stable environments.

• The body never learns to brace, balance, or generate power in unstable situations—things essential for real-world strength.

The Result?

• Weak lower backs, leading to injuries when forced into unexpected positions (like what happened to me)

• Poor athleticism—great in the gym, useless in sports or physical labor.

• Increased risk of injury when moving outside of the gym environment (e.g., tweaking your back when picking something up off the floor).

The Fix:

1. Incorporate instability into training – Think bulgarian split squats, landmine presses, and loaded carries instead of just machines.

2. Train core the right way – Planks, rotation movements, hanging leg raises > crunches.

3. Move athletically – Sled pushes, jumps, rotational work.

Don’t get me wrong, stable machines are great for muscle growth and strength gains - but on a whole, they require less core and stabaliser activation - so doing them in isolation will not create a weaker functioning physique.
